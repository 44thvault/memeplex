WHAT IS A MEMEPLEX?

Ideas don't travel alone. They cluster into self-reinforcing systems — memeplexes — that reshape minds, cultures, and civilizations. This simulator shows you exactly how it happens, one mind at a time.

◈ MEMES & MEMEPLEXES — THE BASICS

In 1976, biologist Richard Dawkins coined the word meme — a unit of cultural information that replicates from mind to mind, just as genes replicate from body to body. Memes include ideas, phrases, rituals, images, and belief structures.

A memeplex (meme-complex) is what happens when memes cluster. The afterlife belief spreads better when combined with sacred texts, divine punishment, and group ritual. They protect each other. Each meme increases the others' survival odds.

Religions, ideologies, conspiracy theories — all memeplexes. Nobody designs them. They self-organize through selection pressure.

◆ HOW TO READ THE SIMULATION

Each little figure on the canvas is a mind. They wander and encounter each other, transmitting memes on contact. Each figure's color shows which meme cluster is dominant in their head.

Thought bubbles appear above figures who are actively infecting someone — you can see the idea jumping between minds in real time.

Glowing rings around figures show memeplex membership — when enough people share the required memes, a memeplex crystallizes and the ring appears.

Bright connection lines between figures show active transmission. Cyan = shared belief reinforcement. White = first contact spread.

Watch for clusters forming — once a memeplex reaches critical mass, it spreads exponentially because carriers are now close together and mutually reinforcing.

◉ KEY PROPERTIES
VIRALITY
How fast a meme spreads on contact. Fear-based and pattern-seeking memes go fastest — they hijack the amygdala before critical thinking engages.
STICKINESS
How long a meme survives once adopted. Unfalsifiable beliefs score 10/10 — evidence bounces off. Resistance to disproof is a survival feature, not a flaw.
RESONANCE
Which cognitive system a meme hooks into: fear, tribal identity, status, existential anxiety, pattern recognition. Higher resonance = faster contagion.
MUTATION
Every transmission introduces small drift. Heresies are mutations. Occasionally one outcompetes the parent — this is how religions split and evolve over centuries.
SELECTION
Memes compete for cognitive bandwidth. Attention is scarce. Social media algorithms now act as artificial selection — optimizing for outrage and novelty.
EMERGENCE
Memeplexes self-assemble through agent interaction and selection. No single prophet designs a religion — the meme cluster finds its own stable form.

◈ THE THEORISTS

Richard Dawkins (The Selfish Gene, 1976) — coined "meme." Cultural transmission follows evolutionary logic: variation, selection, inheritance. The meme doesn't care about your wellbeing any more than the gene does.

Susan Blackmore (The Meme Machine, 1999) — the self is a memeplex, not a pre-existing container. Introduced "temes" — technology-based memes that replicate independently of human minds.

Daniel Dennett — religions are wild memes that evolved to hijack human cognitive systems the same way parasites hijack biology. The host experiences it as meaning; the meme is just replicating.

CCRU / Nick Land — hyperstition: fictions that make themselves real through collective belief and action. Accelerationism as a meme designed to detonate capitalism by speeding it up.

Jonathan Haidt — mapped the moral "hooks" that make memes sticky: care, fairness, loyalty, authority, purity, liberty. These are the resonance categories in this simulator.

◆ WHY THIS MATTERS NOW

For most of human history, memeplex spread was rate-limited by geography and literacy. A new religion might take centuries to achieve dominance. The internet collapsed that to years. Social media collapsed it further — to months or weeks.

Nobody designed QAnon. It was an evolutionary event: memes found a stable self-reinforcing configuration and spread because they matched the cognitive architecture of millions experiencing status anxiety and institutional distrust. The anonymous "Q" was a feature — it made the memeplex more viral because believers projected their own desires onto it.

Understanding memeplexes is cognitive self-defense. When you can see the structure operating — afterlife + divine punishment + chosen people + cosmic enemy — you can recognize the attractor before it fully captures you. That recognition is sometimes the only exit.
