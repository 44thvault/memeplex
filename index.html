What is a Memeplex?

Ideas don't travel alone. They cluster into self-reinforcing systems that reshape minds, cultures, and civilizations. This simulator shows you exactly how it happens, one mind at a time.

◈  Memes & Memeplexes — The Basics

In 1976, biologist Richard Dawkins coined the word meme — a unit of cultural information that replicates from mind to mind, just as genes replicate from body to body.

A memeplex is what happens when memes cluster for mutual survival. Afterlife spreads better alongside sacred texts, divine punishment, and ritual. They protect and amplify each other.

Religions, ideologies, conspiracy theories — all memeplexes. Nobody designs them. They self-organize through selection pressure.

◉  Key Properties
Virality
How fast it spreads on contact. Fear-based memes go fastest — amygdala hijack bypasses critical thinking.
Stickiness
How long it survives once adopted. Unfalsifiable beliefs score 10/10 — evidence bounces off.
Resonance
Which cognitive system it hooks: fear, tribalism, status anxiety, existential dread, pattern recognition.
Mutation
Every retelling drifts. Heresies are mutations — occasionally one outcompetes the parent meme.
Selection
Memes compete for cognitive bandwidth. Social media algorithms now act as artificial selection.
Emergence
Memeplexes self-assemble without designers — only selection pressure required.
◆  The 7 Memeplexes — Field Guide
◈  The Theorists

Richard Dawkins (The Selfish Gene, 1976) — coined "meme." Cultural transmission follows evolutionary logic: variation, selection, inheritance.

Susan Blackmore (The Meme Machine, 1999) — the self is a memeplex, not a container. Introduced "temes" — tech-based memes replicating independently of human minds.

Daniel Dennett — religions are wild memes that evolved to hijack cognitive systems exactly as parasites hijack biology.

CCRU / Nick Land — hyperstition: fictions that make themselves real through collective belief and action.

Jonathan Haidt — mapped the moral hooks that make memes sticky: care, fairness, loyalty, authority, purity, liberty.

◆  Why This Matters Now

For most of history, memeplex spread was limited by geography and literacy. A new religion might take centuries. The internet collapsed that to years. Social media collapsed it to months.

Nobody designed QAnon. It was an evolutionary event: memes found a stable self-reinforcing configuration matching the cognitive architecture of millions under status anxiety. Anonymous "Q" was a feature — believers projected their own desires onto it, making it more viral.

Understanding memeplexes is cognitive self-defense. When you can see the structure — afterlife + divine punishment + chosen people + cosmic enemy — you can recognize the attractor before it fully captures you.

Sources & Further Reading

The theory behind this simulator. Every mechanic — virality, stickiness, memeplex formation, mutation — is grounded in published research.

FOUNDATIONAL TEXTS
The Selfish Gene
Richard Dawkins · 1976
The origin of the word "meme." Dawkins argued cultural units replicate, compete, and evolve by the same logic as genes — variation, selection, inheritance. Chapter 11 introduces the meme concept directly. The single most important book for understanding this simulator.
ORIGIN OF MEMETICS
The Meme Machine
Susan Blackmore · 1999
Extends Dawkins into a full theory of mind. Blackmore argues the self is itself a memeplex — a cluster of memes that creates the illusion of a unified "I." She introduces "temes": technology-hosted memes that replicate independently of human minds.
MEMETICS · MIND
Darwin's Dangerous Idea
Daniel Dennett · 1995
Applies evolutionary logic to culture, language, and religion. Dennett argues memes colonize brains like parasites, and that religions are self-replicating systems that evolved to survive — not to serve human interests.
EVOLUTION · RELIGION
Breaking the Spell
Daniel Dennett · 2006
Applies the meme framework specifically to religion as a natural phenomenon. Why do religions have the specific features they do — afterlife promises, in-group loyalty demands, unfalsifiable claims, divine punishment? Because those features maximise replication, not truth.
RELIGION · MEMETICS
MORAL PSYCHOLOGY & TRIBAL COGNITION
The Righteous Mind
Jonathan Haidt · 2012
Maps the six moral foundations that act as meme-hooks: care, fairness, loyalty, authority, purity, liberty. The "resonance" mechanic in this simulator is directly based on Haidt's framework.
MORAL PSYCHOLOGY
Grooming, Gossip, and the Evolution of Language
Robin Dunbar · 1996
Language evolved to maintain social bonds in large groups. The proximity-based transmission model in this simulator reflects Dunbar's social contagion framework.
LANGUAGE · SOCIAL NETWORKS
ACCELERATIONISM & HYPERSTITION
Fanged Noumena
Nick Land · 2011 (essays 1987–2007)
The theoretical core of CCRU. Land develops hyperstition — the concept that certain fictions become real through collective belief and action. The "memhazard" and "accel" memes in this simulator are drawn directly from this framework.
CCRU · HYPERSTITION
CCRU Writings 1997–2003
Cybernetic Culture Research Unit · 2017
Collected output from the Warwick University theory collective. Introduces the Numogram, Lemurian time-sorcery, and a rigorous account of how recursive cultural systems bootstrap themselves into existence.
CCRU · NUMOGRAM
CONSPIRACY, RADICALIZATION & INFORMATION WARFARE
A Lot of People Are Saying
Russell Muirhead & Nancy Rosenblum · 2019
Analysis of "new conspiracism" — conspiracy without theory, assertion without evidence. The Q-TYPE COMPLEX in this simulator models this architecture.
CONSPIRACY · POLITICS
This Is Not Propaganda
Peter Pomerantsev · 2019
Documents how modern information warfare operates not by promoting one narrative but by flooding the zone — creating epistemic chaos so that nothing feels certain.
INFORMATION WARFARE
SIMULATION & AGENT-BASED MODELING
Emergence
Steven Johnson · 2001
The theoretical basis for agent-based modeling. Complex group behavior — including ideological clustering — emerges from simple local rules. No agent "knows" about the memeplex forming; it self-assembles from individual interactions.
EMERGENCE · COMPLEXITY
The Tipping Point
Malcolm Gladwell · 2000
Popularized the threshold model of social contagion. The "critical mass" and "dominance" notifications in this simulator are modeled on tipping point thresholds — 30% and 50% of population.
CONTAGION · THRESHOLDS
This simulator is an educational model. The memeplexes shown are analytic categories drawn from academic memetics and social psychology — not value judgments. The same framework that explains religious formation explains conspiracy formation explains Silicon Valley eschatology. The point is the structure, not the content.
