What is a Memeplex?

Ideas don't travel alone. They cluster into self-reinforcing systems — memeplexes — that reshape minds, cultures, and civilizations. This simulator shows you exactly how it happens, one mind at a time.

◈  The Basics: Memes & Memeplexes

In 1976, biologist Richard Dawkins coined the word meme — a unit of cultural information that replicates from mind to mind, just as genes replicate from body to body. Memes include ideas, phrases, rituals, images, and belief structures.

A memeplex (meme-complex) is what happens when memes cluster together for mutual survival. The afterlife belief spreads better combined with sacred texts, divine punishment, and group ritual. They protect and amplify each other.

Religions, ideologies, conspiracy theories — all memeplexes. Nobody designs them. They self-organize through selection pressure.

◆  How to Read the Simulation

Each figure on the canvas is a mind. They wander and infect each other with memes on contact. A figure's color shows which meme is currently dominant in their head.

Thought bubbles appear when a mind successfully transmits an idea to someone else — you can watch the idea jump in real time.

Glowing rings show memeplex membership — when a figure carries all the required memes for a memeplex, their ring lights up in that memeplex's color.

Connection lines between figures show active transmission. Cyan = shared belief reinforcement. Dashed white = first-contact spread.

◉  Key Properties
Virality
How fast a meme spreads on contact. Fear-based and pattern-seeking memes go fastest — they hijack the amygdala before critical thinking kicks in.
Stickiness
How long a meme survives once adopted. Unfalsifiable beliefs score 10/10 — evidence bounces off. Resistance to disproof is a survival feature, not a flaw.
Resonance
Which cognitive system a meme hooks into: fear, tribalism, status anxiety, existential dread, pattern recognition. Higher resonance = faster contagion.
Mutation
Every transmission introduces small drift. Heresies are mutations. Occasionally one outcompetes the parent — this is how religions split and evolve.
Selection
Memes compete for cognitive bandwidth. Attention is scarce. Social media algorithms now act as artificial selection — optimizing for outrage and novelty.
Emergence
Memeplexes self-assemble through interaction and selection. No single prophet designs a religion — the meme cluster finds its own stable configuration.

◈  The Theorists

Richard Dawkins (The Selfish Gene, 1976) — coined "meme." Cultural transmission follows evolutionary logic: variation, selection, inheritance. The meme doesn't care about your wellbeing any more than the gene does.

Susan Blackmore (The Meme Machine, 1999) — the self is a memeplex, not a pre-existing container. Introduced "temes" — technology-based memes that replicate independently of human minds. Your phone is a teme machine.

Daniel Dennett — religions are wild memes that evolved to hijack human cognitive systems exactly as parasites hijack biology. The host experiences it as meaning; the meme is just replicating.

CCRU / Nick Land — hyperstition: fictions that make themselves real through collective belief and action. Accelerationism as a meme designed to detonate capitalism by speeding it up.

Jonathan Haidt — mapped the moral hooks that make memes sticky: care, fairness, loyalty, authority, purity, liberty. These are the resonance categories in this simulator.

◆  Why This Matters Now

For most of human history, memeplex spread was rate-limited by geography and literacy. A new religion might take centuries to achieve dominance. The internet collapsed that to years. Social media algorithms collapsed it further — to months or weeks.

Nobody designed QAnon. It was an evolutionary event: memes found a stable self-reinforcing configuration and spread because they matched the cognitive architecture of millions experiencing status anxiety and institutional distrust. The anonymous "Q" was a feature — believers projected their own desires onto it, making the memeplex more viral, not less.

Understanding memeplexes is cognitive self-defense. When you can see the structure operating — afterlife + divine punishment + chosen people + cosmic enemy — you can recognize the attractor before it fully captures you. That recognition is sometimes the only exit.
